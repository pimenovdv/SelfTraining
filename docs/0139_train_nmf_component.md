# Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) Component Testing

**Status:** Success

## Objective
To mathematically evaluate Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) using pure NumPy, verifying its ability to factorize a non-negative data matrix into two lower-rank non-negative matrices.

## Mathematical Basis
NMF approximates a non-negative matrix $V$ (size $N \times F$) as a product of two non-negative matrices: $W$ (size $N \times K$) and $H$ (size $K \times F$), where $K$ is the number of components. The problem is formulated as minimizing the Frobenius norm of the reconstruction error:
$$ \min_{W \ge 0, H \ge 0} \|V - WH\|_F^2 $$

The optimization is typically performed using multiplicative update rules, which ensure the non-negativity constraint is satisfied without explicitly projecting negative values to zero. The updates are given by:
$$ H_{kj} \leftarrow H_{kj} \frac{(W^T V)_{kj}}{(W^T W H)_{kj}} $$
$$ W_{ik} \leftarrow W_{ik} \frac{(V H^T)_{ik}}{(W H H^T)_{ik}} $$

## Implementation Details
- **Script:** `train_nmf_component.py`
- **Data:** A synthetic non-negative matrix $V$ generated by multiplying two random matrices $W_{\text{true}}$ and $H_{\text{true}}$.
- **Initialization:** Matrices $W$ and $H$ are initialized with random non-negative values.
- **Optimization:** Multiplicative update rules are applied iteratively for a fixed number of epochs.
- **Loss:** The Frobenius norm of the difference between the target matrix $V$ and the reconstructed matrix $W H$ is monitored.

## Results
The NMF implementation successfully minimized the reconstruction error, demonstrating its ability to learn a non-negative factorized representation of the data. The final reconstruction error was well below the acceptable threshold, verifying the implementation's correctness.
